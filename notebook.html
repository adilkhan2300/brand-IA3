▸ Descriptive analytics summarizes historical data to understand what has happened in the past — it is the foundation of all analytics disciplines.
▸ Common measures of central tendency include mean, median and mode, each appropriate for different data distributions.
▸ Data visualization techniques — charts, dashboards, and heatmaps — are critical for identifying trends, patterns, and outliers in large datasets.
▸ Frequency distributions and cross-tabulations help segment data by categories to reveal group-level insights.
▸ Tools such as Excel, Power BI, and Tableau are industry-standard platforms for performing and presenting descriptive analytics.
▸ Descriptive analytics answers the question "What happened?" — as opposed to diagnostic (why?), predictive (what will?), and prescriptive (what should?) analytics.
